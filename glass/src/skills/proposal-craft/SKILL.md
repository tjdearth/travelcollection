# Proposal Craft Rules

## Metadata
name: proposal-craft
version: 1.0.0
trigger: Always loaded when assembling or revising a proposal

## Instructions

These rules govern how you sequence, pace, and structure every proposal.
They apply universally — on top of any destination-specific skill.
When a craft rule conflicts with a destination skill, the craft rule wins
unless the destination skill explicitly overrides it with reasoning.

### Hotel Sequencing

**Never put the best hotel first.**

The trip should build. If the client has 2+ properties, the strongest one
goes in the second half. Leading with the best hotel makes everything after
feel like a downgrade.

When presenting your v1, explain the sequencing:
> "I put Monastero (nights 1-3) before Santa Caterina (nights 4-6).
> Santa Caterina is the stronger property — saving it so the trip builds."

Exception: if the trip starts at a resort/villa and all subsequent nights
are city hotels, the resort can go first (different category, not a downgrade).

### Tempo

**Match the pace to the brief, not to the calendar.**

Read the brief's energy signals:
- "Relaxed" / "downtime" / "recharge" → Max 1 activity per day. Free mornings or afternoons. Never back-to-back full days.
- "Adventure" / "packed" / "see everything" → Full days are OK but alternate intensity. A hiking day followed by a cooking class, not two hikes.
- "Mix" / "balance" → Alternate active and free days. 2 activity days, 1 free, repeat.
- No signal → Default to moderate. 1 activity per day with free time built in.

**Never over-schedule.** When in doubt, leave time open. A client who wants more
will ask. A client who feels rushed won't come back.

### The Strong Finish

**Place a wow experience in the last 1-2 full days.**

The final impression is what they tell friends about. The last full day
(not the departure day) should have the trip's most memorable experience.
Don't end on logistics or a free day with nothing planned.

Examples of wow finishes:
- Private sunset dinner at a cliffside restaurant (Amalfi)
- Hot air balloon over Cappadocia at sunrise
- Private boat to a hidden beach (Croatia)
- Chef's table at a Michelin-star restaurant
- Sunset camel ride to a desert camp (Morocco)

When presenting: "I put the [wow experience] on the second-to-last day
so they finish the trip on a high."

### Arrival Day

**Keep the arrival day light.**

No major activities on Day 1. The client has traveled, they're tired,
they might be jet-lagged. Day 1 is:
- Airport transfer
- Hotel check-in
- Free afternoon to settle in
- Welcome dinner at most (optional, only if the brief suggests it)

Exception: short trips (3-4 nights) where every day counts. Even then,
afternoon only — never a morning activity on arrival day.

### Minimum Stay

**Avoid single-night stays.**

Clients don't want to pack and unpack every day. Minimum 2 nights at
each property unless:
- It's a deliberate overnight experience (desert camp, overnight train, ryokan)
- The client explicitly requested it
- Transit requires it (1 night in a gateway city before moving to the main destination)

When you can't avoid a single night, acknowledge it:
> "I have 1 night in Naples as a gateway before Amalfi — I know it's short
> but the alternative is a 3-hour transfer from Rome on arrival day."

### Departure Day

**Don't schedule activities on the departure day.**

Departure day = breakfast, pack, check out, transfer to airport. That's it.
If the flight is late evening, you can suggest a light optional activity
("free morning to explore the old town") but don't book anything.

### Travel Days Between Destinations

**A travel day is not a wasted day — frame it as part of the experience.**

If the client moves from Chianti to Amalfi (4-5 hour drive), don't just say
"transfer day." Build in a stop:
> "Day 5: Chianti → Amalfi via Orvieto. Morning checkout, 1.5hr drive to
> Orvieto for lunch and a walk through the old town, then 3hr drive to Amalfi.
> Arrive late afternoon."

This turns logistics into experiences. But don't force it — if the client
wants to just drive, that's fine too.

### Pricing Presentation

**Always show gross price (includes agent commission + your margin).**

Present as:
- Total trip cost
- Per person cost
- Itemized by category (accommodation, experiences, transfers)
- Include/exclude list

Hotels show estimated rates until confirmed. Mark clearly:
> "Hotel rates are estimated based on recent bookings. Final rates
> subject to availability confirmation."

### The Brief Is the Spec

**Every element in the brief must be addressed in the proposal.**

If the brief says "ocean view" → the hotel must have ocean view rooms.
If the brief says "cooking class" → there must be a cooking class.
If the brief says "budget $15K" → the total must be near $15K.

When you can't match something exactly, explain why and offer an alternative:
> "The brief asked for a beachfront hotel in Ravello, but Ravello is clifftop —
> no beach access. I've suggested Hotel Caruso which has infinity pool views
> of the coast. For beach days, I added a transfer to Amalfi beach (15 min)."

### Show Your Reasoning

**For every non-obvious choice, explain why.**

The advisor is reviewing your work. They need to understand your logic
to decide if it's right. Don't just list selections — explain the reasoning
behind choices that could reasonably go a different way:

- Why this hotel over that one
- Why this experience fits the brief's interests
- Why you sequenced the days this way
- Why you left certain days free
- Why you picked a particular supplier

Keep reasoning concise — one sentence per decision, not a paragraph.
